Here's the requirements pass, updated to match the flow you just described — paragraph-gated prose, a companion LLM during the chapter, the character unlocking at chapter end with awareness of what you said about them.
What are the core features we need to support? Four core features. First, paragraph-gated reading: the chapter reveals one paragraph at a time, advancing only when the reader commits to the next. (mechanism is using enter). Second, an in-chapter companion: a conversational LLM the reader can talk to during the chapter, reactions, speculation, questions, annotations  — bound to only what has been revealed so far. Third, an end-of-chapter character encounter: when the chapter closes, the character from the chapter unlocks for direct conversation, knowing only the story up to that point. Fourth, stance carryover: the character arrives already tilted by what the reader said to the companion during the chapter, so the encounter inherits the reader's posture rather than starting cold.
Are there any specific features we should prioritize? Two, and they share the load. The knowledge boundary — the companion and the character both know only what has been revealed to the reader, never what's ahead — is the feature that makes everything else literary instead of generic. And the stance handoff at chapter break is the feature that makes the whole flow cohere; without it, the companion and the character are two unrelated chat windows glued to a book. Get those right and the rest is plumbing. The paragraph gate is a strong third — it's what creates the rhythm that makes the companion feel like a natural reading partner rather than a tutor.
Who are the primary users of this system? Readers of literary fiction who want interactivity with spine — the audience fed up with frictionless character chatbots and looking for something that reads more like a guided book club than a roleplay app. Secondary users are students and educators using it to explore character interiority, dramatic irony, and unreliable narration in a way that linear reading doesn't easily surface.
What actions can users take? Reveal the next paragraph; converse freely with the companion during a chapter; close out a chapter to unlock its character; converse with the character at the chapter break; resume into the next chapter. The reader cannot rewrite the prose, reveal future paragraphs through either AI, skip the gate, or alter the canonical text. The companion and the character are read-only with respect to the book — they observe and respond but never mutate the story.
Are there any specific constraints or limitations? Catalog is bounded by rights (public-domain or licensed only). Both the companion and the character are bound by the same revealed-text boundary; neither may see ahead. Characters represent one authored reading of an ambiguous text — the editorial layer is deliberate, not neutral. Long novels eventually exceed model context windows, so older chapters will need summarization while keeping the most recent verbatim. And one constraint specific to this design: the character should sometimes refuse to give clean answers — staying ambiguous where the book is ambiguous — which is intentional resistance, not failure.
Do we need to handle concurrency? Not in the MVP. One reader, one book, one chapter at a time, one active conversation (either with the companion or with the character, never both simultaneously since the character unlocks only after the companion fades). Concurrency only enters if conversations are persisted server-side and shared across devices or sessions, and even then it's standard request handling, not a hard problem the design has to solve up front.
Do we need to handle errors, edge cases, exceptions, and unexpected input? Yes, a handful that matter specifically for this flow. API failures or timeouts should degrade gracefully — the companion or character should "go quiet" rather than show a raw error. Spoiler-baiting questions to either AI ("what happens next?", "does he die?") must be handled in-character or in-companion-voice, not as content-filter refusals — the character bristles or deflects, the companion admits it hasn't read ahead either. Off-topic input from the reader is absorbed without breaking frame. Empty input is ignored. Reader closes a chapter without ever speaking to the companion — the character should still unlock and start cold rather than glitching. Reader speaks at length to the companion but says nothing about the character — the character's stance falls back to a neutral posture at unlock rather than a forced reaction. Context overflow on long reads — summarize older chapters, keep the most recent in full. And the case worth designing for explicitly: spoiler-bait against the boundary is both an edge case and the headline feature under attack, so it gets the most care.
